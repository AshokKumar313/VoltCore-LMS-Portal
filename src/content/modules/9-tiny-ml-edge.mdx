## SECTION I: MATHEMATICAL FOUNDATION OF POST-TRAINING INT8 QUANTIZATION

In mission-critical industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) deployments, executing 32-bit single-precision floating-point ($FP32$) neural network forward passes consumes prohibitive memory bandwidth and dynamic power ($P_{\text{dyn}} = \alpha C V^2 f$). Microcontrollers such as the ESP32 (Xtensa LX6/LX7) and ARM Cortex-M4F possess constrained SRAM (typically 128 KB to 512 KB). Deploying Deep Neural Networks (DNNs) on bare-metal firmware requires **Post-Training Quantization (PTQ)**, transforming continuous real-valued weights and activations into compact 8-bit signed integers ($INT8 \in [-128, 127]$).

### 1.1 Asymmetric Affine Quantization Formulation

Let a real continuous tensor value be denoted by $r \in [\alpha, \beta] \subset \mathbb{R}$. The affine asymmetric quantization mapping to an 8-bit integer $q \in [\mathbb{Z}_{\min}, \mathbb{Z}_{\max}]$ is defined by:

$$q = \text{clamp}\left( \left\lfloor \frac{r}{S} \right\rceil + Z, \; \mathbb{Z}_{\min}, \; \mathbb{Z}_{\max} \right)$$

Where:
- $S \in \mathbb{R}^+$ is the arbitrary positive real **Scale Factor**, representing the real-world step size corresponding to one integer unit:
  $$S = \frac{\beta - \alpha}{q_{\max} - q_{\min}} = \frac{\beta - \alpha}{255}$$
- $Z \in \mathbb{Z}$ is the integer **Zero-Point**, ensuring that the real number $0.0$ maps exactly to a representable integer value without rounding error:
  $$Z = \text{round}\left( -\frac{\alpha}{S} \right) + q_{\min}$$
- $\lfloor \cdot \rceil$ denotes the nearest integer rounding operator (Round-to-Nearest-Even).

The inverse dequantization transformation approximating the original real tensor is:

$$\hat{r} = S \cdot (q - Z)$$

### 1.2 Quantized Matrix Multiplication Derivation

Consider the inner product between an input activation vector $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^K$ and a weight filter column $\mathbf{w} \in \mathbb{R}^K$, producing an output activation $y \in \mathbb{R}$:

$$y = \sum_{k=1}^K x_k \cdot w_k + b$$

Substituting the quantized representations $\hat{x}_k = S_x (q_{x,k} - Z_x)$, $\hat{w}_k = S_w (q_{w,k} - Z_w)$, and bias $\hat{b} = S_b (q_b - Z_b)$ (where conventionally $S_b = S_x \cdot S_w$ and $Z_b = 0$):

$$S_y (q_{y} - Z_y) = \sum_{k=1}^K S_x (q_{x,k} - Z_x) \cdot S_w (q_{w,k} - Z_w) + S_x S_w q_b$$

Factoring out the scale products:

$$S_y (q_{y} - Z_y) = S_x S_w \left[ \sum_{k=1}^K (q_{x,k} - Z_x)(q_{w,k} - Z_w) + q_b \right]$$

Dividing both sides by $S_y$:

$$q_y = Z_y + M \cdot \left[ \sum_{k=1}^K (q_{x,k} q_{w,k} - Z_w q_{x,k} - Z_x q_{w,k} + Z_x Z_w) + q_b \right]$$

Where $M$ is the combined real multiplier:

$$M = \frac{S_x S_w}{S_y}$$

### 1.3 Fixed-Point Multiplier Representation ($M_0 \cdot 2^{-n}$)

Because microcontrollers without hardware floating-point units cannot execute the real multiplier $M \in (0, 1)$ without severe software emulation latency, $M$ is decomposed into a normalized 32-bit fixed-point mantissa $M_0 \in [0.5, 1.0)$ and an integer right-shift exponent $n \in \mathbb{Z}^+$:

$$M = M_0 \cdot 2^{-n} = \frac{M_{\text{quantized}}}{2^{31}} \cdot 2^{-n}$$

Where $M_{\text{quantized}} = \text{round}(M_0 \cdot 2^{31})$. The multiplication is executed using high-speed integer ARM SIMD instructions (`__SMMULR` or `SMULL`), completing in a single clock cycle:

```c
// High-Speed Fixed-Point Quantized Multiplication in C
int32_t MultiplyByQuantizedMultiplier(int32_t val, int32_t M_quantized, int shift) {
    int64_t total = (int64_t)val * (int64_t)M_quantized;
    int32_t rounded = (int32_t)((total + (1LL << 30)) >> 31);
    return rounded >> shift;
}
```

## SECTION II: VIBRATION SPECTRAL FEATURE EXTRACTION VIA RADIX-2 COOLEY-TUKEY FFT

Vibration acceleration signals sampled from industrial rotational machinery (such as induction motors, coolant pumps, and UAV drone brushless ESC actuators) manifest characteristic acoustic and mechanical spectral signatures.

### 2.1 Discrete Fourier Transform & Harmonic Leakage

For an $N$-point time-domain sequence $x[n]$ sampled at frequency $F_s$, the Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT) is:

$$X[k] = \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} x[n] \cdot W_N^{kn}, \quad W_N = e^{-j \frac{2\pi}{N}}, \quad k = 0, 1, \dots, N-1$$

The frequency corresponding to spectral bin index $k$ is given by:

$$f_k = k \cdot \frac{F_s}{N}$$

To eliminate rectangular window spectral leakage (sidelobe spillover of $-13\text{ dB}$ into adjacent frequency bins), a Hann smoothing window $w[n]$ is applied in the time domain before FFT execution:

$$w[n] = 0.5 \left( 1 - \cos\left( \frac{2\pi n}{N-1} \right) \right), \quad 0 \le n \le N-1$$

$$x_{\text{windowed}}[n] = x[n] \cdot w[n]$$

### 2.2 Industrial Ball Bearing Characteristic Defect Frequencies

Rotational defects generate kinetic impulse shocks at periodic intervals governed by bearing internal geometry:

| Fault Modality | Notation | Mathematical Expression | Typical Resonance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Ball Pass Frequency Inner Race** | $\text{BPFI}$ | $\frac{N_b}{2} f_r \left( 1 + \frac{d}{D} \cos \theta \right)$ | $180\text{ Hz} - 320\text{ Hz}$ |
| **Ball Pass Frequency Outer Race** | $\text{BPFO}$ | $\frac{N_b}{2} f_r \left( 1 - \frac{d}{D} \cos \theta \right)$ | $105\text{ Hz} - 195\text{ Hz}$ |
| **Ball Spin Frequency** | $\text{BSF}$ | $\frac{D}{2d} f_r \left( 1 - \left(\frac{d}{D}\cos \theta\right)^2 \right)$ | $60\text{ Hz} - 140\text{ Hz}$ |
| **Fundamental Train Frequency (Cage)** | $\text{FTF}$ | $\frac{1}{2} f_r \left( 1 - \frac{d}{D} \cos \theta \right)$ | $15\text{ Hz} - 25\text{ Hz}$ |

Where $f_r$ is the shaft rotational frequency, $N_b$ is the number of rolling elements, $d$ is the ball diameter, $D$ is the pitch diameter, and $\theta$ is the contact angle.

## SECTION IV: THE ENGINEERING TRAP: INTEGER ARITHMETIC ZERO-POINT OVERFLOW & CACHE LINE THRASHING

> [!CAUTION]
> **Trap Title:** Unclamped Zero-Point Accumulator Overflow & Non-32-Byte Aligned L1 Cache Line Thrashing  
> **Root Cause:** When accumulating dot products of $INT8$ quantized vectors across $K \ge 64$ channels with non-zero zero-points ($Z_x, Z_w$), the intermediate sum can exceed $2^{15}-1$ or $2^{31}-1$. Furthermore, unaligned feature buffer memory access across FreeRTOS task boundaries triggers CPU bus stalls and pipeline flushes.

### 4.1 Mathematical Analysis of the Overflow

Consider the quantized dot product sum:

$$A = \sum_{k=1}^K (q_{x,k} - Z_x)(q_{w,k} - Z_w)$$

In worst-case conditions:
- $q_{x,k} = 127$, $Z_x = -128 \implies (q_{x,k} - Z_x) = 255$
- $q_{w,k} = 127$, $Z_w = -128 \implies (q_{w,k} - Z_w) = 255$
- Each product term: $255 \times 255 = 65,025$

For a layer with $K = 64$ inputs:

$$\sum_{k=1}^{64} 65,025 = 4,161,600 > 2^{21}$$

If the embedded engineer inadvertently uses a 16-bit intermediate accumulator (`int16_t`, bounded at $32,767$), the register **silently overflows into negative values** after merely $\lceil 32,767 / 65,025 \rceil = 1$ multiplication! The neural network subsequently outputs catastrophic classification failures, diagnosing a violently vibrating bearing as completely healthy (`NORMAL_OPERATION`).

Furthermore, placing the FFT input buffer across unaligned memory boundaries causes the Xtensa/Cortex-M L1 data cache controller to issue two memory bus transactions per 32-bit word, causing **Cache Line Thrashing** and degrading DSP throughput by $41.8\%$.

// VERIFIED: Uses 32-bit wide accumulator with hardware saturation and 32-byte alignment.

#define CACHE_LINE_SIZE 32

// Aligned to 32-byte boundary to prevent L1 cache line splitting
alignas(CACHE_LINE_SIZE) static int8_t g_aligned_features[32];

int32_t RobustQuantizedDotProduct(
    const int8_t* __restrict__ x,
    const int8_t* __restrict__ w,
    int K,
    int32_t Z_x,
    int32_t Z_w,
    int32_t M_quantized,
    int shift,
    int32_t Z_y
) {
    // 1. Maintain 32-bit or 64-bit wide signed integer accumulator
    int32_t acc = 0;
    
    // 2. Precompute zero-point offsets to minimize in-loop scalar operations
    int32_t sum_x = 0;
    int32_t sum_w = 0;

    for (int i = 0; i < K; i++) {
        int32_t xi = (int32_t)x[i];
        int32_t wi = (int32_t)w[i];
        acc += xi * wi;
        sum_x += xi;
        sum_w += wi;
    }

    // Mathematical zero-point compensation:
    // acc = \sum (x - Z_x)(w - Z_w) = \sum xw - Z_w \sum x - Z_x \sum w + K * Z_x * Z_w
    acc -= Z_w * sum_x;
    acc -= Z_x * sum_w;
    acc += K * Z_x * Z_w;

    // 3. Fixed-Point Multiplier Scaling with 64-bit precision
    int64_t scaled = (int64_t)acc * (int64_t)M_quantized;
    int32_t rounded = (int32_t)((scaled + (1LL << 30)) >> 31);
    int32_t output = (rounded >> shift) + Z_y;

    // 4. Hardware Saturation Clamping (-128 to 127) using __SSAT
    #if defined(__ARM_ARCH_7EM__) || defined(__ARM_ARCH_8M_MAIN__)
        return __SSAT(output, 8);
    #else
        if (output > 127) return 127;
        if (output < -128) return -128;
        return output;
    #endif
}

## SECTION V: VIVA VOCE & GATE ECE CHALLENGE QUESTIONS

### Question 1: Quantization Zero-Point & Dynamic Range (Anna University ECE Viva)
**Question:** In post-training INT8 asymmetric quantization, under what circumstances can the Zero-Point $Z$ be set exactly to zero ($Z = 0$), and what are the hardware performance advantages?  
**Model Answer:**  
The Zero-Point $Z$ can be set to zero when the dynamic range of the tensor is strictly **symmetric around zero**, such that $\alpha = -\beta$. This converts the mapping into **Symmetric Quantization**:
$$S = \frac{\beta}{127}, \quad Z = 0$$
When $Z_x = 0$ and $Z_w = 0$, the expansion terms $-Z_w \sum x$, $-Z_x \sum w$, and $K Z_x Z_w$ vanish completely:
$$\sum_{k=1}^K (q_{x,k} - 0)(q_{w,k} - 0) = \sum_{k=1}^K q_{x,k} \cdot q_{w,k}$$
This reduces memory access and enables bare-metal microcontrollers to utilize ARM Dual-MAC SIMD instructions (`__SMLAD`), processing two 8-bit multiply-accumulate operations in a single clock cycle without any zero-point correction overhead.

### Question 2: FFT Frequency Resolution & Leakage (GATE ECE Advanced Problem)
**Question:** An ADXL345 accelerometer samples motor casing vibration at $F_s = 1024\text{ Hz}$. An $N = 64$-point FFT is executed.  
(a) Determine the frequency resolution $\Delta f$.  
(b) A bearing exhibits an inner-race defect at $f_{\text{fault}} = 245\text{ Hz}$. Will this fault fall on an exact FFT bin, and what phenomenon occurs?  

**Mathematical Derivation:**  
**(a) Frequency Resolution:**
$$\Delta f = \frac{F_s}{N} = \frac{1024\text{ Hz}}{64} = 16\text{ Hz}$$
Each spectral bin $k$ represents frequency $f_k = k \times 16\text{ Hz}$.

**(b) Fault Frequency Mapping:**
Dividing the fault frequency by the bin resolution:
$$k_{\text{target}} = \frac{245\text{ Hz}}{16\text{ Hz}} = 15.3125$$
Since $k_{\text{target}}$ is **non-integer**, the physical energy of the $245\text{ Hz}$ defect does not coincide with an exact orthogonal basis function of the DFT. This induces **Spectral Leakage**, wherein the energy smears across adjacent bins $k = 15$ ($240\text{ Hz}$) and $k = 16$ ($256\text{ Hz}$), attenuating peak amplitude by up to $3.92\text{ dB}$ unless a Hann smoothing window is applied.

        samples_real[i] = 1.0f * sinf(2.0f * M_PI * 50.0f * t) +
