# Advanced Analog Small-Signal Foundations & High-Frequency Signal Integrity

<div class="ieee-abstract">
  <strong>Abstract</strong>—In sub-nanometer VLSI design and multi-gigahertz edge computing, analog small-signal fidelity and electromagnetic signal integrity govern physical limits. ## I. First-Principles Derivation of the Small-Signal Hybrid-`$\pi$` Model

The collector current of an NPN Bipolar Junction Transistor operating in the active forward region is described by the Shockley relation:


```latex
I_C = I_S \exp\left( \frac{V_{BE}}{V_T} \right) \left( 1 + \frac{V_{CE}}{V_A} \right)
```


Where `$V_T = \frac{k_B T}{q} \approx 25.86  mV$` at $T = 300 K$, and $V_A$ is the Early voltage.

### 1. Transconductance ($g_m$)
Taking the partial derivative of collector current with respect to base-emitter voltage at the DC quiescent bias point `$Q(I_C, V_{CE})$`:


```latex
g_m = \left. \frac{\partial I_C}{\partial V_{BE}} \right|_{V_{CE} = const} = \frac{I_S}{V_T} \exp\left( \frac{V_{BE}}{V_T} \right) \left( 1 + \frac{V_{CE}}{V_A} \right) = \frac{I_C}{V_T}
```


### 2. Base Input Resistance (`$r_\pi$`)
Since the small-signal base current is related to collector current by the small-signal common-emitter current gain `$\beta_0 = \frac{\partial I_C}{\partial I_B}$`:


```latex
r_\pi = \left. \frac{\partial V_{BE}}{\partial I_B} \right|_{V_{CE} = const} = \frac{\partial V_{BE}}{\partial I_C} \cdot \frac{\partial I_C}{\partial I_B} = \frac{1}{g_m} \cdot \beta_0 = \frac{\beta_0 V_T}{I_C}
```


### 3. Output Early Resistance ($r_o$)
Accounting for base-width modulation (the Early effect):


```latex
r_o = \left[ \left. \frac{\partial I_C}{\partial V_{CE}} \right|_{V_{BE} = const} \right]^{-1} = \left[ \frac{I_S \exp\left( \frac{V_{BE}}{V_T} \right)}{V_A} \right]^{-1} \approx \frac{V_A + V_{CE}}{I_C} \approx \frac{V_A}{I_C}
```

## II. The Miller Theorem & High-Frequency Pole Splitting

Consider a feedback impedance $Z$ bridging two nodes with voltage gain `$A_v = \frac{V_2}{V_1}$`. The current flowing through the bridging impedance from Node 1 to Node 2 is:


```latex
I = \frac{V_1 - V_2}{Z} = \frac{V_1 - A_v V_1}{Z} = \frac{V_1 (1 - A_v)}{Z}
```


The equivalent admittance seen looking into Node 1 from ground is:


```latex
Y_1 = \frac{I}{V_1} = \frac{1 - A_v}{Z}
```


For a feedback bridging capacitance `$C_\mu$` connecting base (Node 1) to collector (Node 2) with inverting midband voltage gain $A_v = -|A_v|$:


```latex
Y_1 = s C_\mu \left( 1 - (-|A_v|) \right) = s \left[ C_\mu (1 + |A_v|) \right] \implies C_{M} = C_\mu (1 + |A_v|)
```


Thus, even a minute $4 pF$ collector-base capacitance with a voltage gain of $A_v = -200$ appears across the input as:


```latex
C_{M} = 4 pF \times (1 + 200) = 804 pF
```


This massive effective capacitance shifts the dominant input pole down by two orders of magnitude, bounding the $-3 dB$ bandwidth.

## III. Maxwell's Electromagnetic Boundary Conditions on PCB Microstrips

High-speed digital traces operating with sub-nanosecond rise times must be treated as electromagnetic transmission lines governed by Maxwell's curl equations:


```latex
\nabla \times \vec{E} = -\frac{\partial \vec{B}}{\partial t}, \quad \nabla \times \vec{H} = \vec{J} + \frac{\partial \vec{D}}{\partial t}
```


### 1. Tangential Electric Field Boundary Condition
By taking a closed rectangular line integral `$\oint \vec{E} \cdot d\vec{l}$` with loop height `$\Delta h \to 0$` across the interface between the copper conductor (Medium 1) and the FR-4 dielectric substrate (Medium 2):


```latex
\oint \vec{E} \cdot d\vec{l} = (E_{1t} - E_{2t})\Delta w = 0 \implies \hat{n} \times (\vec{E}_1 - \vec{E}_2) = 0 \implies E_{1t} = E_{2t}
```


*The tangential component of the electric field is strictly continuous across the interface.*

### 2. Normal Displacement Field Boundary Condition
By taking an infinitesimal pillbox Gaussian surface across the boundary with thickness `$\Delta h \to 0$`:


```latex
\oiint \vec{D} \cdot d\vec{A} = (D_{1n} - D_{2n})\Delta A = \rho_s \Delta A \implies \hat{n} \cdot (\vec{D}_1 - \vec{D}_2) = \rho_s
```


*The discontinuity in the normal displacement field is exactly equal to the free surface charge density `$\rho_s$`.*

### 3. Skin Depth Formulation (`$\delta$`)
Inside a lossy copper conductor with conductivity `$\sigma = 5.8 \times 10^7  S/m$` and magnetic permeability `$\mu = \mu_0 = 4\pi \times 10^{-7}  H/m$`, current density decays exponentially with depth:


```latex
J(z) = J_0 \exp\left( -\frac{z}{\delta} \right), \quad \delta = \sqrt{\frac{2}{\omega \mu \sigma}} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{\pi f \mu \sigma}}
```


At $f = 2.4  GHz$ (UAV telemetry / Wi-Fi):


```latex
\delta = \sqrt{\frac{1}{\pi \times (2.4 \times 10^9) \times (4\pi \times 10^{-7}) \times (5.8 \times 10^7)}} \approx 1.34  µm
```


Because the current is confined to a $1.34  µm$ outer skin, trace surface roughness (`$R_a \approx 1.5  µm$`) dramatically increases effective AC series resistance.

## IV. Shannon-Hartley Channel Capacity Proof

The maximum error-free data transmission rate $C$ (in bits per second) across an analog communications channel of bandwidth $B$ perturbed by Additive White Gaussian Noise (AWGN) of spectral density $N_0/2$ is bounded by:


```latex
C = B \log_2\left( 1 + \frac{S}{N} \right)
```


### Proof Sketch:
1. Per the Nyquist sampling theorem, a strictly band-limited signal of bandwidth $B$ and duration $T$ is completely specified by $2BT$ independent samples.
2. In a $2BT$-dimensional Euclidean signal space, the received signal vectors have an average energy per dimension of $S + N$. The received vectors are therefore enclosed within an $n$-dimensional hypersphere of radius `$R_{rx} = \sqrt{n(S + N)}$`, where $n = 2BT$.
3. Each distinct transmitted message codeword is surrounded by a noise sphere of uncertainty of radius `$R_{noise} = \sqrt{n N}$`.
4. The maximum number of mutually distinguishable non-overlapping message spheres $M$ that can be packed within the received space is the ratio of their $n$-dimensional volumes:


```latex
M \le \frac{Vol(R_{rx})}{Vol(R_{noise})} = \left( \frac{\sqrt{n(S + N)}}{\sqrt{n N}} \right)^n = \left( 1 + \frac{S}{N} \right)^{n/2} = \left( 1 + \frac{S}{N} \right)^{BT}
```


5. The channel capacity $C$ is the maximum rate of information per second:


```latex
C = \frac{\log_2(M)}{T} = \frac{BT \log_2\left( 1 + \frac{S}{N} \right)}{T} = B \log_2\left( 1 + \frac{S}{N} \right) \quad \blacksquare
```
